# 4. THE PRODUCT — AFTERPACT

## Product identity

**AFTERPACT — The Assurance Layer for Agentic Commerce**

Primary positioning:

"AI agents can execute purchases. AFTERPACT verifies commercial outcomes and coordinates safe, accountable recovery when reality differs from the agreed transaction."

## Problem

AI shopping and procurement systems are increasingly able to:

- Discover products.
- Compare options.
- Negotiate or evaluate offers.
- Request approvals.
- Execute payments.

But these capabilities do not establish that a transaction achieved its intended commercial outcome.

A successful payment can still result in:

- Missing products.
- Incorrect quantities.
- Unexpected price changes.
- Missed delivery commitments.
- Incomplete services.
- Unresolved disputes.
- Duplicate operational actions.
- Failed compensation attempts.
- Inconsistent reconciliation.
- Poor auditability.

## Proposed solution

AFTERPACT introduces a post-payment assurance control plane.

Every supported purchase becomes a verifiable lifecycle:

**Intent → Offer → Structured Promise → Approval → Payment → Outcome Verification → Exception Detection → Authorized Resolution → Verified Financial Result**

This is the essential product.

Do not reduce AFTERPACT to:

- A shopping chatbot.
- A generic payment dashboard.
- A fraud score generator.
- A simple refund button.
- A generic multi-agent framework.
- A budget-limited purchasing assistant.

## Initial customer segment

Prioritize merchant operations teams and marketplaces responsible for handling transaction exceptions.

Secondary segments:

- Enterprise procurement.
- AI commerce platforms.
- SaaS vendors operating transaction agents.
- Retailers.
- B2B service marketplaces.

Do not attempt to simultaneously build a full product for every segment.

Use one clear initial customer persona, while maintaining extensible architecture.

# 5. THE SEVEN CORE PRODUCT ENGINES

## Engine A — Intent Intelligence

Accept a natural-language purchasing request.

Example:

"Find the best equipment for my design studio under $1,000, with delivery before Friday."

Extract:

- User intent.
- Budget.
- Currency.
- Product categories.
- Quantity.
- Delivery deadline.
- Merchant constraints.
- Risk tolerance.
- Mandatory conditions.

Use structured schema validation.

Distinguish user-provided requirements from AI inference.

Never fabricate product availability or seller guarantees.

## Engine B — Offer Discovery

Integrate Channel3 for real product discovery.

Support:

- Product search.
- Price comparison.
- Merchant identification.
- Product attributes.
- Source attribution.
- Ranking explanations.
- Missing-data handling.

Important:

A Channel3 search result does not automatically imply that the merchant can be charged using our own PayPal merchant credentials.

Separate product discovery from the supported payment execution path.

For the main end-to-end demonstration, use an authorized sandbox merchant and explicitly identified demonstration offer.

## Engine C — Executable Purchase Promises

Create a structured commercial promise from the selected offer.

Example fields:

- Promise ID.
- Buyer identity.
- Authorized merchant.
- Order reference.
- Currency.
- Maximum payable amount.
- Items.
- Quantities.
- Expected delivery date.
- Acceptable tolerances.
- Evidence requirements.
- Resolution policy.
- Approval identity.
- Approval timestamp.
- Version.
- Integrity hash.

Any material change must invalidate the old approval and require reapproval.

A promise is an internal verification object, not a claim that AFTERPACT creates a legally enforceable smart contract or regulated escrow.

## Engine D — Payment Execution

Use actual PayPal Sandbox APIs.

Required baseline:

- OAuth authentication.
- Create order.
- Buyer approval.
- Capture payment.
- Retrieve and reconcile payment status.
- Issue full or partial authorized refunds.
- Retrieve and verify refund status.

Expose each financial operation through tightly scoped server-side tools.

The AI may propose an operation but must not directly control the payment API.

## Engine E — Outcome Verification

Represent post-payment business events.

Sources may include:

- Authorized merchant confirmations.
- Delivery evidence.
- Uploaded documents.
- Service completion events.
- Sandbox demonstration events.
- Structured external integrations.

For the hackathon, simulated logistics events are acceptable if explicitly labeled and never confused with live third-party tracking.

The system must detect mismatches between:

- Purchased vs. delivered quantity.
- Promised vs. observed deadline.
- Agreed vs. actual price.
- Required vs. delivered service.
- Expected vs. recorded commercial outcome.

Include evidence provenance and confidence levels.

## Engine F — Autonomous Resolution Intelligence

When an incident occurs:

1. Gather verified facts.
2. Determine the breached obligation.
3. Classify the incident.
4. Evaluate allowed resolution methods.
5. Recommend a corrective action.
6. Verify financial and authorization constraints.
7. Request necessary human authorization.
8. Execute an authorized action.
9. Verify the financial outcome.
10. Produce an auditable resolution record.

Possible actions:

- Request merchant clarification.
- Request replacement.
- Schedule follow-up.
- Propose partial compensation.
- Recommend a full refund.
- Execute an authorized refund.
- Escalate for manual review.

Do not claim that PayPal provides universal automatic dispute resolution or third-party refunds.

## Engine G — Commerce Flight Recorder

Build a high-quality chronological transaction record.

Capture:

- Original user intent.
- AI recommendation.
- Retrieved offer data.
- Approval.
- Policy evaluation.
- API operation intent.
- PayPal identifiers.
- PayPal status transitions.
- Delivery events.
- Incident evidence.
- Resolution recommendation.
- Human authorization.
- Refund execution.
- Final confirmation.

Each record should be append-only where practicable.

Include tamper-evident hash chaining or another appropriately scoped integrity mechanism.

Never claim that hashing alone makes records legally immutable.

Allow the user to replay the supported state transitions and export a redacted evidence bundle.

This subsystem is a central differentiator.

# 8. SECURITY ARCHITECTURE — NON-NEGOTIABLE

Treat this as financial software.

## AI authority boundaries

The AI must not directly:

- Execute payments.
- Modify merchant recipients.
- Increase approved amounts.
- Bypass policy checks.
- Generate valid approvals.
- Issue refunds without authorization.
- Alter historical financial records.
- Access privileged credentials.

The AI produces structured, reviewable proposals.

Deterministic code validates and authorizes actions.

## Payment integrity

Implement:

- Strong request validation.
- Exact monetary representation.
- Currency-aware calculations.
- Server-side authorization.
- Stable idempotency keys.
- PayPal-Request-Id where supported.
- Replay protection.
- Concurrency controls.
- Transactional state updates.
- Merchant account ownership validation.
- Refund amount limits.
- Approval expiration.
- Approval version binding.
- Webhook signature verification.
- Webhook duplicate handling.
- Provider state reconciliation.
- Secret redaction.
- Structured audit logs.
- Financial kill switch for initiating new operations.

All network uncertainty must be handled without blindly retrying irreversible operations.

A successful HTTP response is not necessarily a successful financial outcome.

Verify status fields and reconcile against PayPal.

Never assume webhook delivery is exactly once.

## Prompt-injection defense

External content is untrusted:

- Product descriptions.
- Merchant statements.
- Uploaded documents.
- Retrieved web pages.
- Tool results.
- Customer-provided free text when used in privileged contexts.

Separate instructions from data.

Validate all generated tool arguments.

Add adversarial tests including:

- Price inflation.
- Merchant substitution.
- Fake preapproval.
- Prompt injection.
- Duplicate refund attempts.
- Replay attacks.
- Cross-tenant access.
- Currency mismatch.
- Expired approval.
- Incorrect refund amount.
- Concurrent approval races.
- Corrupted webhook payloads.

## Financial/legal boundaries

Do not present AFTERPACT as:

- A bank.
- A licensed escrow service.
- A payment processor.
- A guaranteed purchase insurance provider.
- A universal dispute-resolution authority.

Clearly describe the actual service as an orchestration, verification, and authorized-resolution layer.

Use PayPal Sandbox only for the hackathon's payment demonstrations.

Production financial operations must remain disabled unless explicitly designed, reviewed, authorized, and compliant.
